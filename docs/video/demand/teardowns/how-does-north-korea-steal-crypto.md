# Teardown: The Hacker Who Stole $1.5 Billion In 2 Minutes
Lume, 557k views, 3.0x its median, 306 days. https://youtu.be/C5eoTRUZzCo

Read beside it: Johnny Harris, "How North Korea Pulled off a Billion $$$
Heist", 2.9M, 18.0x (Short, 53s), and the failure CoinGecko "Why Does North
Korea Love Stealing Crypto So Much?", 3.6k, 0.7x.

## The promise
One heist, told start to finish as true crime: how a country cut off from
the world took $1.5 billion. The viewer is not crypto-aware; they came for a
heist story, and the crypto is the setting.

## The opening (promise / proof / plan, with times)
- 0:00-0:31 a cold open on a single victim at a job interview, a file that
  has to be run. Scene first, no context.
- 0:31-1:02 the reveal of who is on the other end (Lazarus) and the stake
  ($1.5B). Promise restated at ~0:50.
- Proof: none. It is narrated certainty. The named victim and his story are
  not in any public report we know of; much of the drama is invented.
- Plan: "Let's back up. Who are these people?" at 1:29 - a one-line plan.

## The structure (chapters, points, length, wpm, the turn, the CTA)
22:34, 151 wpm, 11 chapters titled like scenes ("The Interview", "The
Infiltration", "The Perfect Moment", "The Discovery", "The Laundering
Machine", "The Crack"). Chronological: origin of the unit, the way in, the
signing, the discovery, the laundering race, a mistake, the aftermath. The
turn is 5:00-6:13: every signer checked the address, saw a correct screen,
and approved - "multisig working exactly as designed". Ends on "they're
accelerating", no CTA beyond the channel.

## Rewatched and skipped (heatmap)
- Peaks: 0:00 and 0:41 (the file is opened, the reveal), **4:58** (the
  routine transfer every signer verified), **7:54** (the address is not
  theirs - the highest point of the video), **17:50** (the laundering mistake
  that gave investigators a lead).
- Troughs: **9:29-12:12**, all of "The Weight" - four minutes of invented
  inner life for the CEO, almost no replays. Drama with no mechanism in it is
  the part people skipped.

## What satisfied viewers (comments)
Two things recur: the paradox (a country "stuck in the past" pulling off
the biggest heist; Johnny Harris's top comments are 28k and 17k likes on
exactly that) and "the malware beat people's eyes, not the security". One
calls the single-hacker title clickbait. Nobody thanks it for a fact they
could check.

## The failures beside it
CoinGecko 0.7x opens on statistics (two 2026 heists, the $6.75B total) and
the *why* question, then spends its first minutes on counterfeit dollars.
Same subject, more accurate, no single heist told through. Radio Free Asia
0.9x and Cybernews 0.1x are news recaps of totals. **The standouts tell one
theft as a sequence of moments; the failures summarise many.**

## Our version - keep, cannot have, replaced by, rule limits
- **Keep:** one heist told as a sequence (Bybit, 21 Feb 2025), the turn at
  the signing screen, the laundering as a race against freezes, and the
  paradox as the curiosity under it. Chapter cards named like stakes.
- **Cannot have:** an invented victim, an invented CEO monologue, a 22-minute
  runtime. Every person, number and date comes from the article's sources
  (FBI, Chainalysis, Elliptic, TRM, the UN panel, the MSMT report).
- **Replaced by:** drawn mechanism - the multisig screen showing one thing
  and signing another, a `timeline` of the heists at their real spacing,
  a `reel` through the laundering hops, the real FBI attribution wording as a
  `post`. Then what Lume never gives: the three ways in, the two 2026 heists
  that show where it is heading (Drift, Bitget), and what it means for an
  ordinary holder.
- **Rule limits:** no financial advice and no rating of exchanges. "Bybit
  covered its losses" is a sourced fact, not an endorsement. "Attributed to"
  wherever attribution is an agency's or a firm's finding; Drift is "likely,
  not confirmed". The weapons-funding figures are UN-panel estimates and are
  called estimates.
