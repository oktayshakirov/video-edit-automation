## The promise
"Bitcoin: Scam, Ponzi, or Something Bigger?" - the sceptic's word, checked.
The viewer has heard "Bitcoin is a Ponzi" and half believes it; awareness is
problem-aware (they know the claim, not the definition). The description
restates it as "the biggest Ponzi of all time, or the most misunderstood
invention", and promises the definition, real cases and a side-by-side.

## The opening (promise / proof / plan, with times)
Intro 0:00-0:40: the claim and the plan. By 0:40 it is defining the term -
the definition is the proof, not the presenter.

## The structure (chapters, points, length, wpm, the turn, the CTA)
9:32, seven chapters: What is a Ponzi scheme? (0:40) - Famous examples,
Madoff and BitConnect (1:44, the longest at 3 minutes) - Why Bitcoin seems
like a Ponzi (4:43) - What makes Bitcoin different (6:26) - Side-by-side
comparison (7:29) - Final verdict (8:40). **The turn is the steelman**: it
gives the sceptic's case its own chapter before answering it. The close is
a side-by-side table, then the verdict. CTA: coaching, an exchange and a
hardware-wallet affiliate link.

## Our version - keep, cannot have, replaced by, rule limits
- **Keep:** the definition first, named Ponzis as the measuring stick, the
  sceptic's case given its own chapter, a side-by-side that ends on a
  checklist (E24's shape - `stablecoin-interest`).
- **Cannot have:** a presenter, 9 minutes (we run 2:30-3:00, E24 is not
  E26), his verdict ("sound money") and his affiliate links.
- **Replaced by:** the SEC's own definition on its own page; drawn money
  flows (a new `ponzi` beat: the same machine with and without someone in
  the middle); the sceptics' published words as `post` cards, not clips.
- **Rule limits:** the verdict judges the *claim* against a definition,
  never the asset. The sceptic's true part - Bitcoin pays nothing, so a
  seller only gains from a later buyer - is said plainly and left standing.
  No "so it's safe", no "sound money", no direction.
