## Why these

The 11-02 `outliers "is tinnitus in the ear or the brain"` search returned 12
standouts out of 15, the densest page the map has. Besides these two:
House Institute "5 Facts" 2274x, Beckman Institute "Tinnitus, blood flow and
the brain" 1128x, Treble "What Is Tinnitus?" 240x, Dr. Berg "The Real Cause of
Tinnitus (It's Not in Your Ears)" 4.8x. UCI is the cleanest match to the
question; Treble is the comparable channel for a long form (educational, no
patient on camera).

## The promise

Where the sound actually comes from. The viewer has been told "it's your
ears" (or "nothing can be done") and suspects that is not the whole story.
Problem-aware, looking for an explanation they can believe. The UCI title is
the question verbatim; Treble's is the same promise as a reveal ("the truth
about ... your brain").

## The opening (promise / proof / plan)

- **UCI:** sentence 1 is the title, aloud. Sentence 2 at ~0:02 is a "both"
  that turns into a verdict by 0:08 - starts in the ear, mostly the brain.
  Then one more beat: what makes it *loud* is attention. Proof is the white
  coat. No plan.
- **Treble:** no restated promise at all - it opens on ear anatomy, and the
  heatmap punishes it (see below). Proof is the presenter's title and, late,
  that she has tinnitus herself.

## The structure

- **UCI:** three claims in 77 words at 185 wpm, 25 seconds. Ear starts it,
  brain makes it, attention makes it loud. It stops; heatmap peaks at 0:00 -
  it loops.
- **Treble:** 16:05, 3,250 words at 202 wpm, a recorded webinar, eight
  chapters: ear anatomy, sound to brain, hair cells, hearing tests, the nerve,
  the brain's filter, the emotional response, treatment. The turn is at
  ~9:15 - the relay that decides whether a signal matters marks this one as
  danger. Ends on "there is hope" and a consultation offer.

## Rewatched and skipped

- **UCI:** peak at 0:00-0:01 only. The whole video is the hook.
- **Treble:** troughs at 0:19 and 2:06-2:35 (0.00-0.06) - the anatomy tour
  is skipped. **Peaks at 11:26-11:36 and 13:41-14:00** - the danger-tag and
  stress response, then the part about the brain being able to change its
  response. People came for the brain's reaction and the "it can change",
  not for the cochlea.

## What satisfied viewers

- Treble's top comment (54 likes) thanks it for "a coherent explanation"
  instead of "there's nothing we can do". Several more say "finally a good
  explanation", "makes so much sense". One complains it was "way too much
  information". So: **the explanation itself is the relief**, and it has to
  be short.
- Commenters on both recount the brain-side evidence from their own lives:
  the sound vanishing during a frightening phone call, gone in a dead-silent
  room for one, gone while asleep, louder when focused on at night.
- UCI's top comments (272, 125, 28) are anger that there is no real
  treatment and that doctors "don't have a clue". The disappointment we
  cannot fix and must not pretend to.

## The failures beside it

The page has almost none: Berg's 6-day-old Short at 0.9x is too young to
judge. The pattern is that the subject itself travels. Within the page,
the anatomy-first long forms are the ones whose viewers skip - Treble's own
heatmap is the negative evidence. Lead with the brain, keep the ear to one
beat.

## Our version - keep, cannot have, replaced by, rule limits

- **Source:** `the-role-of-neural-pathways-in-tinnitus-perception.mdx`
  (2024). Not the 2023 `what-role-does-the-brain-play-in-tinnitus`, whose
  sections promise "relief" through nutrition.
- **Keep:** the typed question as sentence 1; the three-step answer (starts
  in the ear, made by the brain, made loud by attention and stress); the
  brain's reaction as the part people came for; the "it can change" finish
  as a description of research on how the brain responds, not a promise.
- **Cannot have:** a clinician on camera, a presenter with tinnitus.
- **Replaced by:** the mechanism drawn - a signal path from ear to cortex,
  the gain knob turned up, the night loop (`diagram(loop=True)`); the
  imaging research named as the proof.
- **Rule limits:** never "it's all in your head" - the brain making the sound
  is not the viewer imagining it, and the script says so. "Can change"
  describes how the brain's response works, never that the viewer's sound
  will go quieter. Red flags and an ear specialist stay in. No anatomy tour.
- **Length:** ours ~3-4 minutes against 16; the Short at UCI's three claims
  plus one turn, 40-50s.
