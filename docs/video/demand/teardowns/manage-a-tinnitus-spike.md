# Teardown: How to Manage a Tinnitus Spike (And What Causes Them)

Treble Health, 35,087 views, 5.8x its long-form median (6,090), published
2020-12-04. https://youtu.be/JczVD8fdwXc
Transcript: `youtube-audit/research/transcripts/2020-12-04__how-to-manage-a-tinnitus-spike-and-what-causes-the__JczVD8fdwXc.md` (local only)

The first teardown, 2026-10-25, written to show the method on a real case.

## Why this one

The 10-25 `outliers "tinnitus spikes"` search found **"how to manage a
tinnitus spike"** standing out on three channels: Julian Cowan Hill 44x (an
11-year-old video, so its ratio is inflated against today's channel), Treble
Health 5.8x, Still Tinnitus 3.9x ("How to Stop a Tinnitus Spike When..."),
plus Alice Moyle 7x on the same promise told as her own experience. That is a
proven subject by the 2+ channels rule. This is the Treble one because it is
the comparable channel to ours (educational, no patient on camera).

## The promise

What to do when a spike hits, with the causes as the second half. The viewer
is **in it or afraid of the next one**: problem-aware, wanting relief now,
not theory. The title's lead word is *manage*, an action, and the cause is
in brackets as a bonus.

## The opening (promise / proof / plan)

- **Promise:** restated in the first sentence, at 0:00, nearly word for word
  the title.
- **Plan:** in the same breath, the three parts (what causes it, how to
  prevent it, how to manage it). Done by ~0:10.
- **Proof:** never stated as a credential in the opening. It is carried by
  the channel (audiologists, named in the description) and by "a viewer asked
  for this", which signals the video answers a real person's question.
- **The core idea arrives by 0:20:** calm the nervous system and the
  auditory system follows. Everything after is that idea applied.

## The structure

- 14:27, about 2,250 words at 156 wpm, talking head, six chapters: overview,
  two types of spike, finding triggers, two worked cases, techniques, close.
- **Two kinds of spike defined early (~1:00):** a short one that peaks and
  drops inside 20-30 seconds, and a longer one. This is reassurance placed
  first: most spikes pass.
- **Two worked cases (5:12, 6:47):** an office worker whose afternoons in a
  quiet room set it off, a man whose stress and late nights at home did.
  Each ends in one concrete change (a 5-minute walk, a sleep cut-off).
- **The techniques are a list of about eight**, each tied to the one idea:
  belly breathing, a walk, stretching, exercise, soothing sound through a
  speaker or hearing aids, saying a calming line to yourself, calling
  someone, hot-then-cold shower.
- **The end asks the viewer which technique they will try** and points to
  the next video (their group coaching). The CTA is a give: help with the
  isolation the video just named.

## What satisfied viewers

The top comments (49, 19, 16 likes) are viewers listing **their own**
triggers and techniques - breathing, white noise from an air purifier, rain
sounds, avoiding news at night, a calming phrase. Several thank it for being
calm and practical, "better than the ENT". So the satisfaction is three
things: practical steps, a calm voice, and hope that it passes. Nobody
thanks it for the causes.

## The failures beside it

From the same search, same subject, under 1x:

- Sound Relief, "Tinnitus Spikes Explained & What You Can Do When..." 0.5x.
- Treble Health's own "Prevent Tinnitus Spikes in These 6 Everyday
  Situations" 0.4x, the same channel and expert, a prevention angle.
- Rewiring Tinnitus, "Tinnitus Spikes: A Helpful Reminder..." 0.5x.
- **Ours:** "Why Does Tinnitus Spike?" (`why-does-tinnitus-spike.py`,
  long and Short), 26 views at the 09-04 teardown. Built around *why*.

The pattern: **"explained", "prevent" and "why" lose; "manage", "stop" and
"get through" win.** The demand is for the moment of the spike, not the
mechanism behind it. Same subject, same experts, a different promise.

## Our version - keep, cannot have, replaced by, rule limits

- **Source:** `why-does-tinnitus-spike.mdx` already has the sections this
  needs: "What to do during a spike", "Trigger-by-trigger actions",
  "When to seek help". So this is a **re-angle of an article we already
  covered**, not a new one.
- **Keep:** the in-the-moment promise; the core idea stated in the first 20
  seconds; the two kinds of spike early, as reassurance; a short list of
  concrete steps, each tied back to the one idea; an end that offers the
  next useful thing - our sessions on `/zen` are exactly the "soothing
  sounds" the comments name.
- **Cannot have:** an audiologist on camera, a viewer who asked for it,
  real patients.
- **Replaced by:** the research the article cites, shown on screen as the
  proof (`post` cards for a study's line); the two cases as drawn
  situations, not as people we claim to know.
- **Rule limits:** "manage" here means what to do and how to get through
  it, never a promise that the volume drops. "When to seek help" stays in.
  Title candidates in that register: "What to Do During a Tinnitus Spike",
  "How to Get Through a Tinnitus Spike". Not "How to Stop a Tinnitus Spike",
  which promises an outcome.
- **Length:** ours run 3-5 minutes against their 14. The list is the part
  to keep whole; trim the cases, not the steps.
