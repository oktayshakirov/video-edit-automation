## What satisfied viewers (comments)
Reassurance and speed ("so many times lately you've had to put out a
video to keep us all from having panic attacks"). **The open question in
the comments is ours:** "As long as you check the device screen (clear
signing), the attack should be detected before approval?" The video says
yes - matching the device screen to the computer screen "would thwart
this kind of substitution attack". That is the overclaim: the script also
rewrote addresses in the page text (StepSecurity, Optimizely MVP write-up),
so the page, the paste and the device would all show the same attacker
address. Another commenter's fix (paste from a saved note, compare digit
by digit) works only because the note is a source the page did not touch.

## Our version - keep, cannot have, replaced by, rule limits
- **Keep:** the viewer's own stake in the title (Ledger, "your crypto"),
  the early answer that the device itself was not breached, the "it ran
  without a click" surprise, and the sources in the description.
- **Cannot have:** a trusted presenter, ten minutes of reassurance, the
  affiliate links.
- **Replaced by:** the one thing nobody draws - where the address was
  swapped (the page, the copy, the paste) and why the device screen
  agreed with it. The correction is the video: check against a source
  the page never touched.
- **Rule limits:** no advice on which wallet to buy or whether to hold;
  no claim that anyone on Ledger's site lost money (no source says so);
  the take is "about $7,000" (Report URI via Decrypt $7,150, StepSecurity
  $7,040) and is said as small. The attack vector is unsettled (signup
  flow / impersonation / email guessing), so the video says only "a flaw
  in Optimizely".
