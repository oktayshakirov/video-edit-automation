# Teardown: If you find random Crypto in your wallet... DO THIS NOW
Cyber Scrilla, 235k views, 11.7x its median (2026-11-05 `outliers`, "crypto
airdrop scam"), published 2026-01-16. https://youtu.be/5p3VFGnMClg
Transcript: youtube-audit/research/transcripts/2026-01-16__if-you-find-random-crypto-in-your-wallet-do-this-n__5p3VFGnMClg.md (local only)

## The promise
The viewer's own moment, not a category: a token they did not buy has just
appeared, and they want to know what it means and what to do. Problem-aware
and a little scared. The title never says "scam" or "airdrop" - those are
the answer, not the search.

## The opening (promise / proof / plan, with times)
Promise at 0:00 ("have you ever received a random crypto... someone is
trying to scam you"), the three-scam plan by 0:20, then a full "in this
video I'm breaking down" preview to 0:31. No proof step (the channel is
the proof). The first real payoff is the **reassurance at 0:31**:
receiving a token does not mean the wallet is compromised - anyone can send
to an address, like an email.

## The structure (chapters, points, length, wpm, the turn, the CTA)
9:02, 1,897 words, **210 wpm** (fast). Five chapters: how they find you,
what an airdrop scam is, then three scams ordered least to most dangerous,
each closed by its own one-line rule. The honeypot / fake "claim" site (a
signature that is an approval, not a fee) at 2:08; address poisoning with
the $68M case at 3:10; dusting at 5:07, which spends three minutes on
de-anonymisation and a wrench attack before admitting it is rare now. Ends
on "receiving is harmless, interacting is not" and a plug for another video.
The description leads with a hardware-wallet affiliate link.

## Rewatched and skipped (heatmap)
**Most replayed: 4:58-5:31**, the address-poisoning fix (copy the address
from the source, never from your history) running into the start of
dusting. **Least: 0:16-0:38**, the plan and "in this video" preview - the

## What satisfied viewers (comments)
Thanks are general ("saves heartache"). The useful comments are the
questions it **left open**:
- "How can we block or delete those deposits?" / "just delete it?" - you
  cannot refuse a transfer; you can hide it. Never answered.
- "How can anyone create an address with the start and end mirroring
  another?" - the vanity address is named, never explained. Never answered.
- "This only happens if you don't use a cold wallet" - wrong, and
  uncorrected: a hardware wallet signs whatever its owner approves.
- "If this has happened to me how can I recover any crypto" - twice.
  Unanswered, and exactly where recovery scams find people.
- A long confused comment on how dusting could work if coins "mix" -
  the UTXO point was narrated, not shown.

## Our version - keep, cannot have, replaced by, rule limits
- **Keep:** the moment as the promise; the reassurance as the first real
  payoff (receiving is harmless, interacting is the danger) - it is the
  channel's honest counterintuitive answer; one rule per scam; the
  address-poisoning fix as the climax, since it is what people rewound for.
- **Cut:** the plan preview (the skipped trough) and the long dusting tail.
  Two scams, not three, both from our site: the fake "claim" token
  (`understanding-crypto-airdrops`) and address poisoning
  (`send-and-receive-crypto-safely`).
- **Answer the open comments, in order:** why you cannot refuse it (an
  address is public, a transfer needs no permission); how a look-alike
  address is made (generate keys until the ends match - drawn, with the
  number of tries); why a hardware wallet does not save you (it signs what
  you approve - the "Actually" turn); and the follow-up "recovery service"
  as the last trap (`what-is-a-rug-pull`).
- **Proof replaced by sources on screen:** the May 2024 case (1,155 WBTC,
  about $68M, sent after a look-alike transfer; nearly all returned by May
  10, CoinDesk / Cointelegraph) and the USENIX Security 2025 study (270M
  poisoning attempts on Ethereum and BSC, July 2022 - June 2024; $83.8M
  lost on Ethereum).
- **Cannot have:** a face, a sponsor, their wording. Ours is drawn: the
  approval signature as a `post`, the look-alike addresses side by side.
- **Rule limits:** no wallet, scanner or approval tool recommended by name;
  no value put on any token; nothing about price.
