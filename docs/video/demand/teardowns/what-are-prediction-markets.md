## The promise
What the app everyone heard about on election night actually is and how it
works. The viewer has heard the name and does not know it is crypto.

## The opening (promise / proof / plan, with times)
- 0:00-0:32 the election-night claim: the market called it before the
  networks. A named event the viewer remembers.
- 0:32 the twist under it: its users did not know it ran on crypto.
- Proof: none beyond the anecdote. Plan: "how it works, what makes it
  special, why it's the future" at ~0:50.
- Art of the Problem opens the same way (election night, then a 5000-to-1
  upset) and states its real question at 1:00: *can you trust something
  money can manipulate?*

## The structure (chapters, points, length, wpm, the turn, the CTA)
10:25, 182 wpm, 7 chapters: what it is, how it works, why it is valuable,
its numbers, the airdrop, the future. The mechanism is 1:42-3:15 - a $1
share, 80c/20c, sell any time. Then it turns promotional (retention, volume,
"unbiased", an airdrop, an affiliate link). Ends on a plug for another video.

Art of the Problem builds the mechanism from insurance and a bookie, lands
"the price is a probability" at 10:30, then bounded prices (nobody risks 99c
to win 1c). Ends on the trust question, no answer.

## What satisfied viewers (comments)
"Thanks for not telling me anything useless" - the mechanism was the value.
Top complaints: "gambling with extra steps" (78, the top comment), and the
"unbiased yet skin in the game - a delicious logic pretzel". On Art of the
Problem the top comments are about the *self-correction* (2.7k) and the
complaint that it shows only cases where the market was right (747) and none
where it failed. **The audience wants the mechanism and the honest limits;
the promotional half is what they push back on.**

## Our version - keep, cannot have, replaced by, rule limits
- **Keep:** a named thing (Polymarket) the viewer has heard of; the $1 share
  as the core mechanism, drawn; the trust question as the turn; one real
  dated event in place of the French whale story.
- **Cannot have:** the "unbiased" claim, an airdrop, a referral link, any
  hint that someone made easy money. No odds on any live market.
- **Replaced by:** what both standouts skip and their comments ask for -
  **when the number is wrong, and who decides what happened.** The March
  2025 Ukraine minerals market (about $7M, resolved Yes with no deal signed,
  through a UMA token-holder vote; Polymarket called it "unprecedented" and
  refunded nothing) is the failure case Art of the Problem's viewers asked
  for. Drawn: the $1 share splitting into YES and NO, a price moving as
  money arrives, the resolution flow (proposal, $750 bond, 2-hour window,
  token-holder vote).
- **Rule limits:** no financial advice and no rating of Polymarket or
  Kalshi. "A whale" is what users alleged, not a finding - say "users
  blamed". The article's own risk list (thin markets, vague questions,
  oracle risk, regulation) is the spine of the second half.
