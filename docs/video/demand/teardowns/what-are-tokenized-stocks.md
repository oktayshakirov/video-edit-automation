## Our version - keep, cannot have, replaced by, rule limits
- **Keep:** FSE's promise (do you own the share?) as the long form's
  title half, and its order: what it is, the kinds, the turn ("price
  tracking is not ownership"), then what changes. Kraken's length class
  (~4 minutes) and one chapter per question, not thirteen.
- **Cut:** the "what if" pitch, FSE's eight risks in a row and its
  brokerage-plumbing detour. Three kinds become two the viewer can hold
  (a token with a share behind it, a token with only a price behind it).
- **Answer the open comments:** how it works (drawn: share in a vault,
  token on a chain); dividends (paid by the issuer as more tokens or a
  stablecoin, not by the company - the site article's line); who checks
  the issuer (attestations, the article's checklist); why one got taken
  down (Binance's 2021 stock tokens, which the comment names).
- **Proof replaced by real pages on screen:** OpenAI's 2 Jul 2025 post
  ("These 'OpenAI tokens' are not OpenAI equity") on Robinhood's tokens;
  Kraken's own fine print (no ownership, no voting); the SEC's 17 Sep 2026
  five-year exemption, which requires the same rights as the share,
  including voting, and lets a company refuse.
- **The weekend:** the stock market closes, the token does not - and the
  token's price on a Saturday is set by whoever trades it. The article's
  case (token down 15%, share opens down 7%) is illustrative, not a
  dated event, and is said as "imagine".
- **Cannot have:** a launch-day product, a face. Ours is drawn.
- **Rule limits:** no platform rated or recommended, no "worth it", no
  price, no direction. The question is what you own, never whether to buy.
