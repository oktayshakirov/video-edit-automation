# Teardown: What does tinnitus sound like?

Two standouts on one question, both Shorts, both from clinics:

- MUSC Health, "Ask an Audiologist: What Does Tinnitus Sound Like?" -
  592,882 views, 4820x its Short median (123), published 2023-08-18, 0:11.
  https://youtu.be/OB6tUh1Mb58
- UCI Otolaryngology, "Is tinnitus ringing or buzzing?" - 1,128,945 views,
  932x its Short median (1,212), published 2025-03-19, 0:19.
  https://youtu.be/oyPUBKWbYKY

Transcripts: `youtube-audit/research/transcripts/2023-08-18__ask-an-audiologist-what-does-tinnitus-sound-like__OB6tUh1Mb58.md`
and `2025-03-19__is-tinnitus-ringing-or-buzzing__oyPUBKWbYKY.md` (local only).

## Why these

The 10-28 `outliers "does tinnitus cause hearing loss"` search returned
general explainers instead of answers, and these two sat at the top of it
with the largest ratios on the page. Two channels on one subject is proven by
the map's rule. Both ratios are inflated by tiny channel medians (123 and
1,212 views), so read them as "this question travels in the Shorts feed",
not as the size of the opportunity on a long form.

## The promise

The exact question a person types, answered by a clinician. The viewer is
problem-aware in two different ways: a sufferer checking whether their sound
counts ("is mine tinnitus?"), and a curious or worried listener who has heard
the word. The title is the whole promise; neither thumbnail adds one.

## The opening (promise / proof / plan)

- **Promise:** sentence 1 *is* the title in both (MUSC states it, UCI asks
  it aloud).
- **Proof:** the white coat and the channel name. Nothing is said.
- **Plan:** none. At 11-19 seconds there is nothing to plan.
- **The answer arrives by 0:03.** MUSC lists six sounds in one breath
  (ringing, buzzing, hissing, roaring, thumping, music). UCI defines
  tinnitus, then ties the pitch to where the hearing loss is.

## The structure

One sentence (MUSC, 22 words at 120 wpm) or three (UCI, 68 words at 215
wpm). No beat, no cut, no cards: a person talking to camera. Neither has an
ending; both stop.

## Rewatched and skipped

Both heatmaps peak at 0:00 and nowhere else - the videos loop. At 11 and 19
seconds the whole video is the hook, so the replay is the mechanism, which
is the same finding as `narration.md`'s cold-close rule.

## What satisfied viewers

**Recognition.** The top comments on MUSC (551, 342, 58, 43 likes) are
viewers naming their own sound: audio feedback, a flashbang, a slowed
waterfall that sometimes matches the heartbeat, a mosquito. Under UCI the
same, plus the most-liked objection: "I have no hearing loss" (283, 67, 57,
57). The thing that disappointed: "people know how it sounds, they want to
know how to get rid of it", and "we need a solution".

So the satisfaction is being named, and the disappointment is two things we
can answer honestly and one we cannot: UCI implies tinnitus means hearing
loss, which the article contradicts (many people test as normal); and a
heartbeat sound is mentioned by commenters with no word on whether it
matters, which the article's red flags cover. A cure we do not offer.

## The failures beside it

Not yet looked at as a set. On the same page, the generic "what is tinnitus"
long forms with large views (Fauquier ENT 97x, House Institute 2285x) are a
different promise - causes and treatment - and none of them is about the
sound itself.

## Our version - keep, cannot have, replaced by, rule limits

- **Keep:** the typed question as sentence 1; the sounds named early and in
  plain comparisons; a loop on the Short.
- **Cannot have:** a clinician on camera.
- **Replaced by:** the sources named as the proof (NHS, Mayo Clinic, NIH -
  the article's own), and the sound drawn rather than described: the
  `spectrum` sounds mode, one peak gliding low to high as each is named.
- **Add what they lacked:** the normal-hearing-test answer to UCI's top
  objection; the two kinds that keep time with the body; the heartbeat
  sound as the one worth a check.
- **Rule limits:** no sound tied to a diagnosis; the pitch-to-hearing-loss
  claim UCI makes is not in our article and is not repeated. The sound was
  first drawn, never played - a played ringing is the last thing a
  hyperacusis viewer at 2am needs. The feed check below reversed that.
- **Length:** the Short runs ~45s against their 11-19, because a list of
  five sounds with nothing after it is all they have and our Short has the
  turn (the body sounds). The long form runs ~3:30.

## Feed check, 2026-10-28

The long form's query, "what does tinnitus sound like", searched in the
built-in browser:

- **It is a search people make, and a big one.** NHS 24 "What does tinnitus
  sound like? Ringing, crackling, buzzing examples" 651k; Treble Health
  "What Does Tinnitus Sound Like?" 1M; HearingTracker 349k; Hearing
  Healthcare Centre 1.5M; Sound Relief's "Tinnitus 7500 Hz Tone" 3.1M.
- **The page's promise is to hear it.** Every top result plays examples
  ("examples", "listen to ten crazy tinnitus sounds", a 7500 Hz tone). A
  drawn-only video makes a different promise, and is weaker on the one
  this query makes.
- **Our first cover lost the eye check.** NHS 24's cover is the same
  question in big type over a person with a hand at the ear - ours was the
  same picture with less authority behind it.

**Decision (the editor, 2026-10-28): play them.** A 1.6s sample of each sound,
quiet, the bed dipped under it, in the pause after its name - after a
lower-your-volume warning said and shown on the notice card, in both cuts
(`docs/video/audio.md`, "Sound samples"). The drawn spectrum stays, with
each sample playing while its peak is up.
