## Why this one

The 11-10 "tinnitus" branch found it at 138x, and a 2026-11-12 search for
"what makes tinnitus worse" (yt-dlp, 15 results) proved the subject across
three channels: Treble Health "Tinnitus Getting Worse? STOP These 7
Mistakes" 202k (~19x on the map's 10,839 median), Timpanogos "Are You
Making Tinnitus WORSE Without Realizing it?!" 4.2k (~9x on 443), Cut the
Noise with Dario "5 Things Making Tinnitus Worse, Do This Instead!" 19k
(median not measured).

## The promise

You are doing something, without knowing it, that turns the volume up. The
viewer already has tinnitus, has noticed it moves, and wants a list to
check their own day against. Problem-aware; the hidden lever is "things you
may not be aware of", which makes it about *their* habits.

## The opening (promise / proof / plan)

- **Proof at 0:00**: the audiologist's name and clinic, said first. We
  cannot have this.
- **Promise at ~0:05**: "five things making it louder that you may not be
  aware of".
- **Plan**: none spelled out; a "what is tinnitus" recap, then a like and
  subscribe ask, then the red flags. **The heatmap is near zero from 0:45
  to 1:03** - exactly the subscribe ask and the start of the red flags.

## The structure

1,354 words at 180 wpm, seven chapters: red flags, then five numbered
causes (caffeine and nicotine, medications, alcohol and salt, poor sleep,
untreated hearing loss), then a recap of all five. Each cause is one
mechanism sentence and one "notice it, adjust it" instruction. There is no
turn: the list is presented as settled. Ends on a subscribe ask.

## Rewatched and skipped

- **Peaks land on the start of each numbered item**: 2:38 (1.00,
  medications), 3:05 (alcohol and salt), 4:22 (sleep), 5:21 (hearing
  loss). People skip to the next item. The list *is* the product.
- Trough 0:45-1:03: the subscribe ask and the plug.

## What satisfied viewers, and what did not

- A 326-like comment is the list typed out. They wanted the five names.
- **The warning, in the comments themselves:** "From the comments it
  looks like everything under the sun causes it. We're screwed" (203);
  sugar, wind, keto, GABA - every commenter has their own trigger, and
  half contradict the video ("drinking makes it less noticeable for a
  day for me").
- **"#6 - thinking about it"** (126) and "my tinnitus increased while
  watching this video" (47): attention is the trigger nobody listed.
- Despair is the floor of the thread: decades, no cure, no silence. The
  calm of the video is part of what it is for.

## The failures beside it

Same search, same format, under 1x: Doctor Cliff's own "3 Ways Allergies
are Making Your Tinnitus WORSE" (4.6k) and "Why Your Stress is Making Your
Tinnitus WORSE" (3.2k); Treble's "What makes my tinnitus worse?" (4.3k)
and "STRESS Is Making Your Tinnitus WORSE" (9k). **One named cause loses;
a counted list of several wins, and the plain question loses to it on
Treble's own channel.** The list with a number is the promise.

## Our version - keep, cannot have, replaced by, rule limits

- **Source:** `tinnitus-trigger-tracking.mdx` (2026-07-25), its "triggers
  worth testing" list and its three traps.
- **Keep:** a counted list, each item a reveal (the peaks); the item names
  said plainly so the list could be typed out; the red flags, but short
  and late, never in the opening.
- **Our turn, which theirs does not have and its comments ask for:** the
  list is not equally true. The article sorts it - sleep and stress hold
  up, loud noise needs no testing, caffeine has not shown a link in
  population studies, alcohol probably works through sleep. And why
  everyone's own list is unreliable: tinnitus moves on its own, so a bad
  day always finds a culprit. Attention ("thinking about it") is the
  commenters' #6 and the article's reverse-causation trap.
- **Cannot have:** an audiologist on camera, a clinic.
- **Replaced by:** a `checklist` with verdicts as the proof (the sorting
  is the authority), the night loop drawn, the diary drawn as weeks of
  data where the pattern does or does not appear.
- **Rule limits:** no "cut this and it gets quieter". The article's verbs
  are "test", "log", "worth flagging to a clinician". Medication: never
  "stop taking it" - raise it with your prescriber. The jaw/neck item is
  "worth flagging", not "treatable" as a promise.
- **Skip what they spent the front on:** no recap of what tinnitus is,
  no subscribe ask, no plug.
- **Length:** 2:30-3:00 against 7:32.
