## Formats, and whether they fit this channel

The reason it works comes first; the fit is judged against that reason and
against the medical rule (`projects/tinnitus.md`: describe, never promise
relief).

| format | why its audience clicks | fit here | seen failing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "Does tinnitus cause [fear]? What the research ACTUALLY shows" | a fear they carry, answered by someone who read the research | **yes** - honest answers are the brand | not yet looked |
| "Are [habit] making your tinnitus worse?" / myth-flip | a belief they act on daily is challenged | **yes** - our one Short hit (Silence) is this | not yet looked |
| plain patient question, search words in CAPS (Treble) | it is the exact thing they typed | **yes** for Shorts | ours: "Why Does Tinnitus Spike?" 26 views against their 4.5k - packaging, not subject |
| "If you don't understand [mechanism], you don't understand your tinnitus" | the mechanism is new, the outcome is what they care about | **yes** - promises understanding, not relief. Candidates: auditory gain, habituation, somatic tinnitus | not yet looked |
| "[Named device / treatment]: an honest review" | a decision they are about to spend money on | **careful** - review the evidence, never the user experience we do not have; no ranking of treatments by outcome | not yet looked |
| "BEST [sound] for sleep & tinnitus, N hours" | they need something to play tonight | **yes** for sessions; "relief" in a competitor title is theirs, not a wording we copy | not yet looked |
| "[Name]'s score went from X to Y" | proof that improvement happens to people like them | **no** - needs a real patient and implies a result | - |
| "so fast it feels like cheating" | a wanted result, faster than expected | **no** - a promised outcome | - |
